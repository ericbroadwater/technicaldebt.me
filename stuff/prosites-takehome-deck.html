Thesis

The 8→10% goal is a Homepage problem.

The highest-leverage path from 8% to 10% MQL→Paid runs through one page. With 27,435 sessions converting at 1.68%, the Homepage is the single largest untapped conversion pool on the site. The lead bet: transplant what is working on Premium Content to the Homepage, tested safely via split URL. Every subsequent answer below is in service of that bet or the operating model that keeps it from becoming a one-off.

Part 1A · Web Architecture
Page → CTA → MQL → Salesforce
Page chosen · Homepage
Pick one high-intent page and explain how the experience should work from page request → render → CTA/form → MQL → Salesforce. Cover cookies, browser-side vs. server-side, rendering, FE/BE responsibilities, where HTML/PHP/.NET fit, what data must persist, and the requirements you would give FE dev, BE dev, and IT.

① Render · Browser-side

Request prosites.com/ → DNS → CDN (IT-owned) → origin (WordPress, PHP-rendered).
HTML + critical CSS server-rendered; non-critical JS and web fonts deferred to protect LCP.
First-party cookie set: session ID + UTM captured from URL, mirrored to localStorage as fallback.

② CTA · Browser-side

User clicks Request Demo or Start Free Trial. HTML form, client-side JS validation for UX.
Form POST to first-party subdomain (track.prosites.com) — bypasses most ad blockers.
Server-side re-validation on submit (never trust the client).

③ Processing · Back-end

PHP endpoint writes lead to internal DB; server-to-server call to Salesforce REST API via OAuth token (never client-exposed).
Same endpoint fires server-side GA4 event via Measurement Protocol — the authoritative MQL count lives here, not in client-side gtag.
Response returns lead_id, persisted in cookie for dedupe + return-visit attribution. .NET stack follows same contract, different runtime.
Data that must persist across the flow session ID · UTM source/medium/campaign · first-touch timestamp · form field values · GA4 client_id · lead_id post-submit · Salesforce contact ID — these are the join keys for closed-loop attribution from visit through paid conversion.
Front-end dev
Form semantics, WCAG CTA accessibility, LCP optimization (images, fonts, critical CSS), client-side event wiring, cookie-consent UX.
Back-end dev
Server-side form validation, Salesforce REST integration with retry + idempotency, GA4 Measurement Protocol event emission, structured error logging.
IT
First-party tracking subdomain DNS + SSL, CDN cache rules excluding form endpoints, WAF rules that do not break POSTs, cookie-consent infrastructure.

Part 1B · Safe Rollout
New funnel · Launch discipline
Split URL · Guardrails · Rollback
With the goal of launching a new website/funnel that converts higher than the current one, explain how you would safely test and roll out the new experience. Address rollout approach, success and guardrail metrics, data-quality checks before and after launch, rollback plan, and SEO migration basics.
  • Rollout approach. Server-side split URL test, 50/50 stratified by source (organic / paid / direct). Funnel redesigns touch URL structure — safer to route at the edge than swap components in place. Feature flags inside the new funnel allow CTA iteration without redeploy.
  • Success metrics (ranked). MQL rate (primary); demo rate and trial rate (secondary — mix, not just volume). Read at statistical significance, not first positive signal.
  • Guardrail metrics (must not regress). The brief's three — support tickets per new customer, early churn < 60d, average discounting — instrumented before launch. Plus bounce rate and trial→paid conversion downstream.
  • Data-quality checks, before launch. 100 synthetic form submissions in staging; confirm each lands in GA4 and Salesforce with matching IDs. 404 sweep on internal links. Consent-flow QA. UTM persistence across funnel.
  • Data-quality checks, first 48h post-launch. GA4 event volume vs. baseline (>10% drift = flag); form-to-SFDC parity rate (must be ≥99%); form-error rate in server logs.
  • Rollback plan. Edge routing revert — 10-minute change. Old funnel kept warm for 30 days. Triggers: guardrail breach, or SFDC handoff drops below 99%.
  • SEO migration basics. Preserve URL where possible. If URL changes: 301 map live before DNS cutover, internal links updated site-wide, canonicals preserved, sitemap regenerated + resubmitted. Full checklist · Part 1C.

Part 2A · Patterns in the Data
Jan 2025 – Feb 2026 · CPASS
5 patterns · 3 picks
Using the page data, list 3–5 patterns worth noticing, identify one high-volume opportunity, one high-conversion but lower-volume opportunity, and one underperforming or suspicious page. Explain where you would look first and why.
  • Homepage and Premium Content carry the site. Combined, 74% of sessions and 65% of MQLs. Everything else is noise at the margin.
  • Homepage converts 3.3× lower than Premium Content on equivalent traffic. Same traffic class, 1.68% vs. 5.62%. The gap is intent capture, not audience quality.
  • CTA design is shaping MQL mix, not just volume. Homepage MQLs are 77% free trials; Premium Content MQLs are 93% demos. 40× variance across pages means the CTA surface is doing more work than targeting is.
  • AI Chat (10.28%) and PPC (10.20%) are traffic problems, not CRO problems. Both convert at ~6× site average on under 800 sessions. Optimizing chases diminishing returns; growing traffic is a separate, later bet.
  • Zero-conversion pages are likely intent mismatches, not failures. Secure Firm Portal (322/0) is a post-purchase feature page; LSA (110/0) is informational. Counting them inflates the denominator.

Part 2B · First Conversion Bets
Two bets · Lead with one
Hypothesis · Slice · Kill criteria
Propose two conversion bets for this quarter, then choose one to lead with. For each: hypothesis, why it matters to MQL→Paid, leading indicator watched first, smallest shippable slice, kill criteria.
Lead Bet

Homepage CTA + hero transplanted from Premium Content

Hypothesis
Homepage under-converts because its primary CTA is trial-weighted for an audience that arrived with research intent. Replacing hero + primary CTA with Premium Content's pattern (demo-first framing, specific benefit, adjacent social proof) will lift MQL rate ≥40% without dropping trial volume by more than 15%.
Why MQL→Paid
Demos historically close at higher rates than trials (to confirm in SFDC). Shifting mix toward demos compounds the 8→10% goal — fewer total MQLs could still beat target if close rate rises.
Leading indicator
Demo-request rate on Homepage variant, first read at 48h (directional only; significance not yet reached).
Shippable slice
Swap hero block + primary CTA module on 50% of Homepage traffic. Secondary CTAs and footer unchanged. No below-the-fold changes.
Runtime / size
Baseline 1.68%, MDE 0.8 pt, 80% power → ~14 days at 50% traffic sufficient for detection.
Kill criteria
Demo rate lifts <20% by day 14, or trial rate drops >30%, or any guardrail (tickets, early churn, discounting) breaches.
Runner-up · Run in parallel

MQL definition + Salesforce handoff audit

Hypothesis
Part of the 8% baseline is measurement drift — form submissions not reaching SFDC, duplicate MQL counts, or pages mis-included in rate calc.
Why MQL→Paid
A clean baseline makes every subsequent bet readable. Dirty data makes the Homepage bet's result ambiguous even if it wins.
Leading indicator
Form-to-SFDC parity rate by page; MQL stage dwell time.
Shippable slice
One-week audit by the analyst. Zero eng spend.
Kill criteria
Audit shows <1pt of the 8% baseline is measurement artifact — shelf and move on.
Why not lead
Hygiene investment, not the quarter's growth bet. Run alongside the Homepage test.

Part 3A · Process
Intake · Triage · Work · Launch · Learn
~1 notable change / month
Describe your approach: Intake, Triage, Work Breakdown, Launch Readiness, Post-Launch Learning. Optional: include 1–3 templates or articles that reflect the process style you prefer.
Intake All work enters through one Teams channel #growth-bets. DMs get one reply: "put it in the channel with the template." Required fields: problem, metric it moves, hypothesis, smallest shippable slice, proposed kill criteria. Unfilled templates are prompts, not bets — they go to a holding queue. Bets go to triage.
Triage Weekly, against a public ICE score (Impact × Confidence × Ease, Sean Ellis). Scoring visible in Monday. Stakeholder escalations do not skip scoring. "Never" is a valid answer and is documented — avoids zombie-brief resurfacing.
Work breakdown Maximum one active bet + one in-design bet at a time — matches the ~1-notable-change-per-month constraint. Each bet scoped to a fixed appetite (2 or 4 weeks), set at pitch time. If scope exceeds appetite, scope shrinks — clock does not.
Launch readiness Pre-launch checklist (Part 1C) + event-parity QA. All items green before traffic cutover. Guardrail instrumentation live before variant ships.
Post-launch learning Day-14 decision: continue / adjust / kill / scale. Default is kill unless data says otherwise. Results doc (win or lose) archived in Monday. Losing bets are as valuable as winning ones if the learning is captured.

Part 3C · Tooling Plan
Monday · Teams · Office · Power BI
6 bullets · What must exist · What gets decided
In 6 bullets total, explain how you would use Monday, Teams, Office, and Power BI. Make clear what boards/docs/channels must exist, what brief/decision fields are required, and when Power BI is reviewed and what decisions it drives.
① Monday · Bet Board
Not a brief board. Columns: Thesis, Hypothesis, Appetite, Slice, Status, ICE score, Kill Criteria, Day-14 Decision, Learning Doc link. Two swim lanes: In-Flight, In-Design. Losing bets archived but searchable.
② Monday · Intake Queue
All new requests land here first; cannot move to Bet Board until template is complete and triaged. The firewall against brief-factory drift.
③ Teams · Channels
#growth-bets is the single working surface. #growth-stakeholders (read-only, PM-broadcast) for monthly thesis updates — kills one-to-one DM escalations.
④ Office · Templates
Word: bet brief (1 page), quarterly thesis (2 pages), post-launch results (1 page). Excel: MDE + sample-size calc, published as a reusable sheet so bets are sized consistently.
⑤ Power BI · One Dashboard, Four Tiles
MQL→Paid trend vs. 10% target · Active bet deltas vs. control · Guardrails (tickets per new customer, early churn, discount rate) · Traffic + MQLs by page.
⑥ Power BI · Decisions It Drives
Reviewed weekly in Bet Review (continue/adjust/kill per bet); snapshot exported into Monthly Thesis Review (next-month bet selection) and Quarterly Thesis Lock. If a decision is not traceable to a Power BI view, it is not being made on data.
